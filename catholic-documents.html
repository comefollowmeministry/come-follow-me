Learning from the Church

The Catholic Church teaches through Sacred Scripture, Sacred Tradition, councils, papal documents, the Catechism, Canon Law, and the living Magisterium.

This page provides direct access to official Church documents for prayer, study, formation, catechesis, pastoral ministry, and academic research.

Visitors should read each document carefully, within its historical, theological, pastoral, and ecclesial context.

Catechism of the Catholic Church

The Catechism presents the faith of the Catholic Church in four principal parts:

Profession of Faith

The Creed, revelation, the Trinity, creation, Christ, the Church, forgiveness, resurrection, and eternal life.

Celebration of the Mystery

The liturgy, Sacraments, sacramentals, and the worship of the Church.

Life in Christ

Human dignity, freedom, virtue, grace, morality, the Beatitudes, and the Ten Commandments.

Christian Prayer

The tradition of prayer, expressions of prayer, spiritual struggle, and the Lord’s Prayer.

Catholic Social Teaching

Catholic social teaching applies the Gospel to human dignity, family life, work, economics, politics, peace, justice, solidarity, and care for creation.

Human Dignity

Every human person is created in the image of God and possesses an inviolable dignity.

Common Good

Social conditions should allow persons, families, and communities to flourish.

Solidarity

Human beings are responsible for one another, especially the poor, excluded, and vulnerable.

Subsidiarity

Decisions should be made at the most appropriate level while respecting persons and local communities.

Care for Creation

Creation is a gift entrusted to humanity and must be protected responsibly.

Peace and Justice

Christian disciples are called to promote reconciliation, justice, mercy, and lasting peace.

Guidelines for Study

  • Begin with prayer and ask the Holy Spirit for wisdom and understanding.
  • Read the entire section rather than relying on isolated sentences.
  • Consider the document’s date, purpose, audience, and historical context.
  • Use official Church sources whenever possible.
  • Discuss difficult passages with a priest, catechist, lecturer, or qualified theologian.
  • Distinguish authoritative Church teaching from private interpretation or online opinion.
